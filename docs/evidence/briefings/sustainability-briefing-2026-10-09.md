## 1. Headline: Kering Puts Mycelium in a Maison Bag — and the EU's Greenwash Rules Hit Their First Week

Two stories this week bookend the same question: what can a luxury brand actually claim, and prove, about its materials?

First, Bottega Veneta released a limited collection of small leather goods woven from Ephea, a mycelium-based material from Italian biomaterials startup Sqim — the first time the house has used it, and the first time Kering has put mycelium into a signature intrecciato weave. Kering incubated Ephea through its Material Innovation Lab in Milan, and CEO Luca de Meo has set hard targets: 30% reduction in leather intensity by 2028 versus 2025, and 40% of ready-to-wear from alternative materials by 2035. (Source: https://www.vogue.com/article/your-new-bottega-veneta-bag-could-be-made-from-mushrooms)

Second, the EU's Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (EmpCo) has now been applying since 27 September 2026 — just twelve days ago. Vague terms like "eco-friendly," "green," and "climate friendly" are blacklisted unless a brand can show recognised excellent environmental performance. Carbon-neutral claims built on offsets are banned outright. Self-designed sustainability badges are out. Products already on shelves are not exempt. (Source: https://fashionlawjournal.com/eu-greenwashing-ban-27-september-2026-fashion/)

The strategic read for KAESA: the two stories are not separate. Kering is chasing material innovation precisely because, from 27 September 2026, "sustainable" is a legal claim rather than a marketing flourish. Luxury brands that can name their fibre, their certification and their traceability win on both sides. Everyone else switches to greenhushing.

## 2. Materials & Innovation

- **Fashion for Good launches Cotton Reloop Matrix Project** — a cross-industry initiative with Arvind, BESTSELLER, C&A, Interloop, Reformation, Shahi and Uster to physically validate commercially available recycled cotton yarns against brand specifications. The honest finding behind it: mechanical recycling loses 45–50% of input as short fibre, which caps most yarns at around 10% recycled content before durability suffers. The project will publish a Quality Matrix and an open-source spinning SOP. (Source: https://ww.fashionnetwork.com/news/Fashion-for-good-launches-cotton-reloop-matrix-project-to-unlock-commercial-scale-for-recycled-cotton,1873799.html)

- **Prada launches Re-Cashmere and Re-Wool** — following Re-Nylon, Prada is collecting leftover cashmere and wool from its own knitwear site in Torgiano and reworking it into FW26 pieces. Re-Cashmere is 100% GRS-certified recycled cashmere; Re-Wool blends recycled wool and cashmere with RWS-certified virgin wool. This is the template to watch: closed-loop *within your own production*, which sidesteps feedstock-quality problems entirely. (Source: https://culted.com/fashion/prada-is-giving-cashmere-and-wool-a-new-life/)

- **Scalable mycelium leather moves closer to the line** — researchers published in ACS Applied Bio Materials a submerged-fermentation route (growing *Trichoderma reesei* in brewing-style tanks rather than trays), rolled into sheets 20 cm wide and 8 m long on paper-mill-style rollers. Tensile strength is reported comparable to traditional leather; it disintegrates in water within 28 days and in industrial compost in about six weeks. Tear resistance is the remaining weakness. (Source: https://www.acs.org/pressroom/presspacs/2026/august/your-next-handbag-could-start-with-mushrooms.html)

- **Carbios proves enzymatic PET recycling on industrial lines** — with Patagonia, PUMA and Salomon, Carbios produced recycled polyester T-shirts from complex textile waste on standard European equipment, from repolymerisation in Portugal to knitting and dyeing in France. Fibres matched virgin polyester on mechanical strength, dyeability and colour fastness. (Source: https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/carbios-validates-industrial-scale-production-of-recycled-polyester-t-shirts/)

- **Textile Exchange publishes the first global textile-to-textile feedstock model** — recyclable textile feedstock is projected to grow from ~10 million tonnes (2025) to ~15 million tonnes by 2030, but only 3–5 million tonnes of *spinnable* recycled fibre is likely to actually be available. Expect continued scarcity pricing on recycled inputs. (Source: https://textileexchange.org/news/textile-to-textile-recycled-fiber-processing-capacity-and-availability/)

## 3. Policy & Regulation

- **EmpCo is live and it applies to your weight class** — unlike most EU rules, there is no size-based phase-in. A three-person Seoul studio and a billion-dollar retailer face the same standard, and any brand selling to EU consumers is in scope. A claim about the whole product must be true of the whole product: "made with recycled materials" on a bag with a recycled lining is a problem. (Source: https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/sustainability/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-greenwashing-1239265385/)

- **Carbon-neutral claims are effectively dead for products** — Recital 12 explicitly catches "climate neutral," "CO2 neutral certified," "carbon positive" and "reduced climate impact." You may still describe investment in carbon credit projects, but you cannot convert those credits into a product claim. (Source: https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102o0ge/esg-quick-guide-eu-greenwashing-rules-empowering-consumers-for-the-green-transi)

- **ESPR textile Digital Product Passport: adopted 2027, applied 2029** — the delegated act is still in preparatory study, with adoption targeted Q3–Q4 2027 and mandatory DPP application for textiles flagged for 2029. Near-certain data fields: fibre composition by percentage, country of last substantial transformation, SVHC status, machine-readable care instructions, recyclability and recycled-content percentage, GS1-compatible identifier. (Source: https://dppe.eu/solutions/textiles/)

- **EU textile EPR is coming regardless of the passport** — Directive (EU) 2025/1892 requires member states to transpose textile EPR by 17 June 2027 and have operational schemes by April 2028. Fees will be eco-modulated against durability, repairability and recycled content. Several markets — including the Netherlands, France, Latvia and Hungary — already run live schemes. (Source: https://infodpp.eu/en/industries/textiles/)

- **Unsold-stock destruction ban already in force** — since 19 July 2026, large enterprises cannot routinely destroy unsold apparel, accessories or footwear. Medium-sized enterprises follow on 19 July 2030. This is the clearest regulatory argument yet for smaller production runs. (Source: https://dppe.eu/solutions/textiles/)

- **Korea's ESG disclosure final roadmap moves up and hardens** — the FSC's final plan of 8 July 2026 mandates sustainability disclosure from FY2027 for KOSPI-listed companies with consolidated assets of KRW 10 trillion or more (roughly 291 companies), down from the draft's KRW 30 trillion, and implemented immediately as statutory business-report disclosure rather than via the exchange. Third-party verification from 2030; Scope 3 deferred to 2031. KAESA is well below the threshold today — but your much larger supplier and department-store partners are not, and their Scope 3 reporting will land on you as a data request. (Source: https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026070716191998648)

## 4. Market Intelligence

- **Luxury resale crosses $40bn in 2026** — the luxury resale market grew from $37.95bn in 2025 to $41.61bn in 2026 (9.6% CAGR) and is forecast to reach $60.11bn by 2030. Leather goods are the fastest-growing segment within secondhand luxury, at 11.6% CAGR to $33.4bn. (Source: https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/luxury-resale-global-market-report)

- **Value retention is now a purchase criterion** — myGemma's 2026 index, built on 500,000+ authenticated items, finds Hermès Birkin and Kelly routinely resell above 100% of retail (some new-in-box above 200%), with Chanel Double Flaps around 75%. The headline trend for 2027: buyers evaluating purchases on expected resale value, not just price. For an independent brand, durability and repairability are the two levers that support this narrative. (Source: https://mygemma.com/blogs/news/luxury-resale-index-2026)

- **Handbags are the most-traded resale category** — Hermès models hold three of the top four most-searched bags in 2026 year-to-date, with the Kelly overtaking the Birkin. (Source: https://mygemma.com/blogs/news/luxury-resale-index-2026)

- **The EU market is where the compliance cost sits, and it is not large-brand-only** — because EmpCo has no transition period and no size carve-out, and because EU EPR registration will bind non-EU sellers market by market, EU-bound stock produced under older marketing assumptions carries real risk. Audit your hangtags, packaging and product-page copy before your next EU shipment. (Source: https://softgoodsreport.com/articles/eu-empco-directive-now-applies-raising-stakes-on-green-claims-f37af326)

## 5. For Your Radar

- **"Sustainable" is not on the banned-words list — but treat it as if it were.** EmpCo's Recital 9 lists "environmentally friendly," "eco-friendly," "green," "ecological," "climate friendly," "biodegradable" and "biobased." The word "sustainable" isn't in the recital, but the list is illustrative and a regulator will likely treat an unqualified "sustainable collection" the same way. The safe pattern is the specific fact: "70% organic cotton, certified to GOTS" beats "sustainably made" every time. (Source: https://fashionlawjournal.com/eu-greenwashing-ban-27-september-2026-fashion/)

- **The Green Claims Directive is not law — do not wait for it.** Negotiations stalled in summer 2025 and no further announcements have followed. EmpCo is the operative law; brands holding off on a claims cleanup until the GCD arrives are waiting for the wrong instrument. (Source: https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102o0ge/esg-quick-guide-eu-greenwashing-rules-empowering-consumers-for-the-green-transi)

- **Recover launches RecoverOne** — a 100% recycled cotton/polyester blend in colour-matched ratios that removes the need for downstream overdyeing. Worth a sample request if colour consistency across recycled batches has been a bottleneck for you. (Source: https://www.ecotextile.com/2026100866296/news/materials-production-news/recover-launches-overdye-free-recycled-fibre-blend/)

- **Armedangels has developed a plastic-free bouclé from wool and lyocell** — a useful reference point for anyone building a bag lining or strap that avoids virgin synthetics. (Source: https://fashionunited.com/news/fashion/armedangels-develops-plastic-free-boucle-from-wool-and-lyocell/2026100875108)
