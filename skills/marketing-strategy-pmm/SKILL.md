## When to Use

Use when defining or refining product positioning, building a go-to-market (GTM) plan, running competitive intelligence, defining an ICP, creating sales enablement materials, or planning a product launch. Produces: a positioning statement, ICP profile, GTM plan, battlecards, and a launch checklist. Based on April Dunford's positioning methodology.

## Workflow

### 1. Positioning (April Dunford method)
Answer in order — each answer builds on the last:
1. **Competitive alternatives:** what does the customer do today instead of buying (competitor, workaround, "do nothing")?
2. **Unique attributes:** the 2-3 capabilities/features only you have (or do best).
3. **Value:** the concrete benefit each unique attribute delivers — quantify (time, revenue, risk).
4. **Proof points:** evidence the value is real (case studies, metrics, logos).
5. **Target market:** who feels the pain most acutely.

**Positioning statement template:**
```
For {target customer} who {urgent need},
{product} is a {category} that {key benefit}.
Unlike {competitive alternative}, it {unique attribute → value}.
```
Fill every blank from the five answers; if a blank can't be filled, the work isn't done.

### 2. ICP Definition
Profile: firmographics (industry, size, revenue, geo), role/title and buying committee, pain points (ranked), triggers (events that start a search: regulation, funding, growth spike), budget range, and "why now". Write a 3-5 sentence narrative plus a one-liner ("VP Marketing at mid-market DTC brands, $10-50M revenue, scaling paid channels"). Validate against 3+ real customers (anonymized, e.g., client-mwi) before building GTM around it.

### 3. GTM Plan
1. **Segments:** 1-3 ICP segments, prioritized (segment score = pain severity × willingness to pay × reachability).
2. **Messaging:** per segment — value proposition, 3 key messages max, proof points, objection handling.
3. **Channels:** owned (site, email, content), earned (PR, reviews, partnerships), paid (search, social, ABM). One primary + one secondary channel per segment.
4. **Timeline:** T-90/60 build and validate; T-30 assets + sales enablement; T-14 soft launch to waitlist/early access; T-0 launch; T+30/60/90 review gates.

### 4. Competitive Intelligence (WAR framework)
For each competitor, classify:
- **Win:** where you win and why (your unique attributes vs their weaknesses).
- **Attract:** buyers you pull from them (their churn risk, feature gaps, pricing friction).
- **Repel:** buyers you lose to them (their strengths, your gaps).
Then build the **battlecard**: competitor profile, their pitch, their weaknesses, your counter-messages, proof points, objection responses, and when to engage/disengage. Refresh quarterly.

### 5. Sales Enablement Deliverables
- One-pager (positioning statement + 3 proof points + pricing summary).
- Battlecards for the top 3 competitors.
- Demo script mapped to unique attributes → value.
- Objection handling sheet (top 10 objections with responses).
- Case studies / customer stories (anonymized: client-mwi).
- FAQ + competitive comparison table for the sales team.

### 6. Launch Checklist
- [ ] Positioning statement approved by all stakeholders
- [ ] ICP segments + messaging finalized
- [ ] Landing page / website copy live with consistent messaging
- [ ] Sales enablement pack delivered and trained
- [ ] Press/PR outreach and reviews scheduled
- [ ] Paid and organic channel plan launched
- [ ] Analytics + UTM tracking verified (see paid-ads / campaign-analytics skills)
- [ ] Post-launch metrics and review dates set (T+30/60/90)

## Pitfalls
- Positioning by features instead of alternatives — "we have X feature" doesn't tell anyone why to switch.
- Trying to serve every segment; pick one beachhead segment and win it first.
- Messaging that differs between website, sales deck, and ads — one message everywhere.
- Battlecards that list features without the "how to win" angle.
- Launching with no post-launch metrics plan — no review gates means no iteration.

## Verification
- Positioning statement passes the "blank test": every field is filled with validated (not assumed) answers.
- ICP validated against at least 3 real customers; sales confirms it matches their best-fit deals.
- GTM plan has a named owner, channel, and dates for every segment.
- Sales team confirms the enablement pack answers their top 10 objections (survey or walkthrough).
- Launch checklist items all checked before the launch date.
