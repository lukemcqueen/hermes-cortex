# 🇰🇷 Korean Language Learning — A Companion for Older English Speakers

> *Language learning isn't a race. It's a garden you tend a little each day.*

This skill is for English speakers aged 50+ who want practical Korean — for a Korean spouse, colleagues, living in Korea, or rediscovering heritage. No academic drills. Just real language for real life.

---

## 1. Adult Second-Language Acquisition — Why Your Brain Is Still Brilliant

Older adults learn *differently* — not worse — and in some ways better.

**Your advantages:** Decades of pattern recognition (Korean grammar is deeply systematic). Life experience — when you learn 실례합니다 you already know *when* to say it. Real motivation, real discipline, no cramming.

**What to expect:** Pronunciation takes longer because your mouth has 50+ years of English habit. Memory needs more repetition — that's where Spaced Repetition (section 5) shines. You may feel silly making unfamiliar sounds. Laugh at yourself and keep going.

**The Golden Rule:** 15 minutes daily beats 2 hours weekly. Your brain consolidates language in small daily doses with sleep between them.

## 2. Hangul — Read It in a Week

Hangul was designed by King Sejong (1440s) so *everyone* could learn it — including you.

### The 10 Vowels (Day 1-2)

Vowels build from three shapes: • (heaven), — (earth), | (human).

| Vowel | Sound | Mnemonic |
|-------|-------|----------|
| ㅏ | **a** (f**a**ther) | Vertical + right tick |
| ㅑ | **ya** | Double tick = y + ㅏ |
| ㅓ | **eo** (s**o**n) | Vertical + left tick |
| ㅕ | **yeo** | y + ㅓ |
| ㅗ | **o** (g**o**) | Horizontal above — O mouth |
| ㅛ | **yo** | y + ㅗ |
| ㅜ | **u** (m**oo**n) | Horizontal below — cup shape |
| ㅠ | **yu** | y + ㅜ |
| ㅡ | **eu** (book, unrounded) | Just a horizontal line — flat lips |
| ㅣ | **i** (mach**i**ne) | Just a vertical line |

Write each 10× while saying it aloud. Cover and test yourself.

### The 14 Basic Consonants (Day 3-5)

| Consonant | Sound | Mnemonic |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| ㄱ | **g/k** | Looks like a corner |
| ㄴ | **n** | Tongue on roof shape |
| ㄷ | **d** | Looks like a door |
| ㄹ | **r/l** | Rolling tongue shape — light tap, not trill |
| ㅁ | **m** | Square box — mouth closed |
| ㅂ | **b** | Bucket shape |
| ㅅ | **s** | Hat shape (s for sombrero) |
| ㅇ | **ng / silent** | Circle — silent initially, ng finally |
| ㅈ | **j** | Jet with wing |
| ㅊ | **ch** | ㅈ + extra stroke = extra air |
| ㅋ | **k** (aspirated) | ㄱ + extra line = strong puff |
| ㅌ | **t** (aspirated) | ㄷ + extra line |
| ㅍ | **p** (aspirated) | ㅂ + extra line |
| ㅎ | **h** | Person with hat |

### Reading Blocks (Day 6-7)

Syllables stack: (consonant) + (vowel) + (optional final consonant):
한 = ㅎ+ㅏ+ㄴ → **han**, 글 = ㄱ+ㅡ+ㄹ → **geul**, 한국 (Hanguk), 커피 (coffee), 택시 (taxi).

**Scaffolding trick:** Read everything — shop signs, subway stations, food packages. Understanding comes later. Just sounding out letters is real progress.

## 3. Essential Grammar

### SOV Word Order

Korean is **Subject-Object-Verb** — the verb always ends the sentence.
> English: I eat bread → 저는 빵을 먹어요 (I **bread eat**)

**Drill:** Take English sentences, move the verb to the end. Practise 2 min/day.

### Particles (조사)

| Particle | Role | Rule |
|----------|------|------|
| **은/는** | Topic ("speaking of…") | 은 after consonant, 는 after vowel |
| **이/가** | Subject ("the one who") | 이 after consonant, 가 after vowel |
| **을/를** | Object marker | 을 after consonant, 를 after vowel |
| **에** | To/at (time or place) | 학교에 가요 (go to school) |
| **에서** | At (action location) | 집에서 먹어요 (eat at home) |
| **하고/랑** | And/with | 친구하고 (with friend) |
| **의** | Possession | 제 친구의 책 (friend's book) |

### Verb Conjugation

Dictionary form: **-다** → drop it, add endings based on stem vowel.

| Stem ends in | Add | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ㅏ or ㅗ** | **-아요** | 가다→가요, 보다→봐요 |
| **else** | **-어요** | 먹다→먹어요, 있다→있어요 |
| **하다** | **-해요** | 공부하다→공부해요 |

**Past:** -았/었어요 (갔어요, 먹었어요, 했어요) | **Future:** -을/ㄹ 거예요 (갈 거예요, 먹을 거예요)

### Useful Sentence Endings

| Ending | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| -아/어요 | Polite statement/question |
| -아/어 주세요 | Please do for me |
| -(으)세요 | Polite command |
| -고 싶어요 | Want to |
| -아/어도 돼요? | May I? |
| -네요 | Oh! / I see that… |
| -지요? / -죠? | Right? / Isn't it? |

## 4. Honorifics & Speech Levels

| Level | When | Verb Form |
|-------|------|-----------|
| **하십시오체** | Formal: news, speeches, announcements | 합니다 |
| **해요체** | ✅ **Your default** — polite, warm, never wrong | 해요 |
| **해체** | Close friends, family, younger people | 해 |
| **해라체** | Diaries, books, rules | 한다 |

Only switch to casual 해체 when invited ("말 놓으세요" — drop formalities).

**Honorific -시-:** Insert into verbs to respect the subject: 가다 → 가시다, 하다 → 하시다, 읽다 → 읽으시다.
**Special honorifics:** 있다 → 계시다, 먹다 → 잡수시다, 말하다 → 말씀하시다, 주다 → 드리다.

**Titles:** -씨 (Mr./Ms.), -님 (customer/superior), 선생님 (teacher/any respected adult), 사장님 (boss).

Age determines language. Expect "몇 살이세요?" early — it's not rude, it's practical.

## 5. Spaced Repetition with Anki

**Setup:** Install [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) (free Android/desktop). Use "Korean Grammar Sentences by Evita" deck (1,600+ audio sentences). Set **10-15 new cards/day** — habit over cramming.

**Good card design:**
> ❌ Front: 먹다 → Back: to eat
> ✅ Front: 저는 아침에 밥을 [___] → Back: 먹어요 / 먹다 (to eat)

Full sentences = context = memory anchor. Include audio. One new thing per card.

**Daily 15-min routine:** Morning review with coffee → afternoon new cards → evening pass before sleep. Sleep consolidates memory.

**Stuck on a card?** Delete it. Learn the word in a real sentence, make a better card. Sometimes the card is bad, not your memory.

## 6. Pronunciation

### The Three-Way Consonant System

| Type | Example | How |
|------|---------|-----|
| **Plain (lax)** | 가 [ga] | Soft, relaxed, little air |
| **Aspirated** | 카 [ka] | Strong air burst (paper moves) |
| **Tense** | 까 [kka] | Throat tight, no air, sharp |

**Paper drill:** Hold paper in front of mouth. 가 = barely moves. 카 = flies. 까 = doesn't move, throat tenses. Practice for ㄱ/ㅋ/ㄲ, ㄷ/ㅌ/ㄸ, ㅂ/ㅍ/ㅃ, ㅅ/ㅆ, ㅈ/ㅊ/ㅉ.

### Batchim (Final Consonants)

Many consonants sound the **same** at syllable end:

| Written batchim | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| ㄱ, ㄲ, ㅋ, ㄳ, ㄺ | **-k** (밖→[박]) |
| ㄷ, ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅌ, ㅎ | **-t** (옷→[옫]) |
| ㅂ, ㅍ, ㄼ, ㄿ, ㅄ | **-p** (집→[집]) |
| ㄴ, ㄴㅈ, ㄴㅎ / ㅁ, ㄻ / ㄹ, all ㄹ-clusters | **-n** / **-m** / **-l** |
| ㅇ | **-ng** |

### English Speaker Pitfalls

1. **ㄹ is NOT English R or L.** Light tongue tap on roof of mouth — like the "dd" in "ladder" (US fast speech). Practice: 라라라라라.
2. **Don't aspirate plain consonants.** Beginners say 카 for 가. Difference is *air*, not volume.
3. **ㅡ (eu)** has no English match. Say "book" without rounding lips — lips flat, unrounded.
4. **Double consonants (ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ)** are *tense* — tighten throat, don't lengthen.
5. **Intonation is flat.** Korean doesn't bob up and down like English. End sentences level or slightly lower. Yes/no questions end slightly higher.

## 7. Reading Progression

**Stage 1 — Hangul only (Weeks 1-2):** Sound out everything. Write family names, everyday words.

**Stage 2 — Loanwords (Weeks 2-4):** Read Korean English-loanwords you already understand: 커피, 택시, 버스, 피자, 호텔, 컴퓨터. Coffee shop menus are great practice.

**Stage 3 — Children's books (Months 1-3):** Look for "유아" or "초등 저학년" labels. Pictures carry the story.

**Stage 4 — Webtoons (Month 3+):** Free, visual, conversational. Start with "마음의 소리" (Sound of Heart). [Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com) app in Korean. Guess from pictures — don't translate everything.

**Stage 5 — Simple articles (Month 6+):** TTMIK Iyagi series, KBS Easy Korean (YouTube).

## 8. Cultural Context

**Hierarchy:** Age determines speech level. Older-by-one-year → 형/오빠/누나/언니, not "friend." Always 해요체 until invited otherwise.

**KakaoTalk:** Read receipts ("읽음") visible. Voice messages normal. ㅋㅋ=laughter, ㅠㅠ=crying, ^^=smile. Avoid long messages after 10 PM without closeness.

**Customs:** Accept things with both hands. "글쎄요…" = polite "no" (harmony, not evasion). Pour drinks with both hands for elders; turn head away when drinking before them.

## 10. Practical Scripts

Practice aloud. Record yourself. Compare with native audio.

**☕ Café:** 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요 (one Americano please). 차가운 거 주세요 (cold please). 얼마예요? (how much?). 카드로 할게요 (card). 감사합니다, 맛있게 마실게요! (thanks, I'll enjoy it).

**🚕 Taxi:** 서울역까지 가 주세요 (to Seoul Station). 여기서 세워 주세요 (stop here). 카드 되나요? (card okay?). Also: 천천히 가 주세요 (slow down), 영수증 주세요 (receipt please).

**💊 Pharmacy:** 배가 아파요, 약 좀 주세요 (stomach hurts, medicine please). 머리가 아파요 (headache), 열이 있어요 (fever), 기침이 나요 (cough), 감기약 주세요 (cold meds).

**🥬 Market:** 이거 얼마예요? (how much?). 좀 깎아 주세요 (discount?). 두 개 주세요 (two please). Also: 비싸요 (expensive), 싸요 (cheap), 맛있어 보여요 (looks delicious).

**📞 Phone:** 여보세요? (hello?). 천천히 말씀해 주세요 (speak slowly). 죄송합니다, 한국어 잘 못해요 (sorry, I don't speak well). 다시 한 번 말씀해 주세요 (say it once more). 끊을게요, 안녕히 계세요 (I'll hang up).

**💬 Small Talk:** 저는 [Name]이라고 합니다, 미국에서 왔어요 (I'm [Name], from America). 아니에요, 아직 잘 못해요, 배우고 있어요 (not at all, still learning). 한국어가 재미있어요! 만나서 반갑습니다 (Korean is fun! Nice to meet you).

**🆘 Emergency:** 도와주세요! (help!), 길을 잃었어요 (I'm lost), 병원에 데려가 주세요 (hospital please), 괜찮아요? (are you okay?).

## Ongoing Practice

**5-min daily habits:** Phone in Korean. One webtoon episode. Say three things aloud each morning: "커피 마셔요" (drinking coffee), "날씨 좋네요" (nice weather), "오늘 바빠요" (busy today). Greet your Korean spouse/neighbor/colleague in Korean first.

Every mistake means you're trying. You've already taken the hardest step.
