## When to Use

Use when growing an audience on X/Twitter: writing tweets and threads, optimizing a profile, researching competitors, planning cadence, or analyzing engagement. For multi-platform social content generally, use social-content; this skill adds X-specific depth: algorithm mechanics, thread engineering, reply strategy, profile optimization.

## Workflow

1. **Optimize the profile before any posting.** Four elements, in order:
   - **Bio:** who you are + who you help + proof, under 160 chars. "I turn B2B analytics into decisions. Ex-Gartner. Writing the weekly Numbers That Matter." beats "Digital marketing professional."
   - **Pinned post:** one high-performing piece stating your thesis — the first thing visitors read. Refresh when outperformed.
   - **Header:** reinforces the promise (position, product, or one-line value prop).
   - **Name & handle:** searchable, matching where the audience looks.

2. **Define 3–5 content pillars.** Each is a recurring topic you can produce indefinitely, tied to the audience's job. Examples: (1) lessons from shipping, (2) decision frameworks, (3) data pulls others won't do, (4) behind-the-scenes. Every post maps to a pillar; none fits = noise.

3. **Write threads with a proven structure:**
   - **Hook tweet (tweet 1):** a specific, defensible claim or concrete observation — no "unpopular opinion" clichés, no vague motivation.
   - **Insight tweets (middle):** one idea per tweet, one screenshot-able unit. Build step by step; end each with a forward pull ("the next part is where it gets counterintuitive").
   - **Expansion/CTA (final tweet):** summarize the takeaway, invite replies with a specific question, or link the long-form piece it expands.
   - Keep tweets under 240 characters — room for quote-post commentary.

4. **Set posting cadence.** A sustainable base: 1–3 native posts a day plus 1–2 threads a week. Replies count as posting. Batch-write threads ahead; keep native posts daily between big pieces.

5. **Engage with a reply-first strategy.** Spend as much time replying as posting: answer niche conversations within the first hour (early replies shape a thread's engagement). Quote-post with added value ("This misses X — here's the version with numbers"). Replies are how the algorithm finds you; posts are how they stay.

6. **Measure what matters.** Weekly:
   - **Impressions:** the reach proxy; healthy accounts grow steadily.
   - **Engagement rate:** (likes + replies + reposts + follows) ÷ impressions. 1–3% is normal; under 1% means weak hooks or a mismatched audience.
   - **Profile visits → follows:** high visits, low follows = the profile (bio/pinned) is the leak.
   - **Reply rate on threads:** the best quality signal — threads that get replies are working.

7. **Choose tactics that age well.** Durable: consistent value, reply-first engagement, teaching threads, real data, niche depth. Penalized or dead: follow-for-follow, engagement pods, bought followers, hashtag stuffing, verbatim copies, substance-free bait. Recommend durable tactics; explain why the cheap ones backfire.

## Pitfalls

- Profile last: posting before bio/pinned/header are set wastes impressions.
- Threads written as one long paragraph split by ellipses — every tweet must stand alone.
- Chasing impressions with bait; engagement rate and follows are the real scoreboard.
- Replying only to big accounts; niche conversations are where an audience actually forms.
- Copying what worked for others without the context that made it work (authority, timing, audience).

## Verification

- Can a stranger tell what you do, who you help, and why to follow — from bio and pinned post alone?
- Does every post map to a pillar? Does every thread have hook / insight units / CTA?
- Are impressions, engagement rate, and follow-through named with a data source (X Analytics or the user's tool)?
- Could the account survive 2 weeks on scheduled content alone? If not, cadence is too aggressive.
